Subspace Pruning via Principal Vectors
for Accurate Koopman-Based Approximations

Dhruv Shah, Jorge Cortés
UC San Diego · IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control (submitted) · Conference version: CDC 2026
Paper / CDC version / Code / BibTeX
MPV-SPV fixes the numerical drift of one-at-a-time pruning. Pruning runs from right to left, starting from a large dictionary that contains four known Koopman eigenfunctions. Left: plain SPV drifts away from the known eigenfunctions as it prunes, while MPV-SPV stays about four orders of magnitude closer. Abstract

Finite-dimensional Koopman models such as EDMD are only as accurate as the subspace of observables they are built on. When that subspace is not invariant under the Koopman operator, predictions and eigenfunctions inherit an error that the invariance proximity, the sine of the largest principal angle between a subspace and its Koopman image, bounds in closed form. We study the problem of pruning a large dictionary to a subspace whose invariance proximity falls below a given tolerance. We cast pruning geometrically in terms of principal angles and vectors, show that single-principal-vector (SPV) pruning coincides with Recursive Forward-Backward EDMD, and introduce a batch variant (MPV). In large dictionaries, one-at-a-time pruning suffers from numerical drift: eigenfunctions that lie only approximately in the dictionary are gradually lost. We bound this drift and use the bound to design MPV-SPV, a hybrid that first prunes coarsely and then refines, which keeps known eigenfunctions about four orders of magnitude closer and yields a subspace about 20× closer to invariant. Rank-one updates of the principal angles and vectors make each pruning step cheap, so the total runtime is dominated by a single initial SVD. On the Van der Pol oscillator, a 12-dimensional pruned model tracks the dynamics over 3000 steps where EDMD drifts and kernel EDMD goes unstable.

Walkthrough

1The Koopman operator

  • For dynamics x+ = T(x), the Koopman operator acts on observables by composition: 𝒦f = f ∘ T. It is linear even when T is not.
  • Define a Koopman-invariant subspace (𝒦f ∈ S for every f ∈ S) and an eigenfunction (φ(x+) = λφ(x)).
  • Cite Koopman (1931).

2Eigenfunctions capture system behavior

  • Use the worked example x1+ = 0.8x1, x2+ = 0.5x2 + 0.7x12 + 0.1, which has an exact invariant subspace span(1, x1, x2, x12, x1x2, x13).
  • The eigenfunction table could be an HTML table. Explain the plot: level sets of φ2 and φ4, with trajectories hopping between them.

3Finite-dimensional representations

  • The problem: capture 𝒦 on a finite-dimensional subspace S. The approximation PS𝒦 is exact only if S is invariant.
  • EDMD is the orthogonal projection PS𝒦|S in the empirical L2 inner product over the data.
  • End with the question: how do we measure the quality of a subspace?

4Principal angles and invariance proximity

  • Define principal angles and vectors between two subspaces as successive best-aligned pairs. The 3D figure is a good intuition aid.
  • Key quantity: invariance proximity sin θmax(S, 𝒦S) equals the worst-case relative prediction error over S, and the supremum is achieved (Haseli & Cortés, arXiv:2311.13033).

5The subspace pruning problem

  • State the problem: given Sinit and ε ∈ [0, 1), find an expressive S* ⊆ Sinit with sin θmax(𝒦S*, S*) ≤ ε.
  • Why it matters: reliable prediction of every observable in S*, and reliable eigenfunctions (both follow from the worst-case bound).
  • Remarks: it is a combinatorial search, larger subspaces are preferred, and the idea is to drop the worst principal vectors until the tolerance is met.

6Single principal vector (SPV) pruning

  • Walk through the flowchart: compute principal angles and vectors of (Sk, 𝒦Sk), stop if sin θmax ≤ ε, otherwise remove the worst principal vector and repeat.
  • Properties: worst-offender rule, recomputation every step, and nested subspaces.

7Multiple principal vector (MPV) pruning

  • Same loop, but drop every principal vector with sin θj > ε at once.
  • Trade-off: fewer pruning steps, but it can be too aggressive. Slides 6 and 7 could be merged into one section with a single figure.

8SPV is RFB-EDMD

  • RFB-EDMD (Haseli & Cortés, IEEE Access 2025) prunes the top eigenvector of the consistency matrix Mc = I − KfKb.
  • Lemma: the eigenvalues of Mc are sin2 θi(S, 𝒦S), and its eigenvectors map to the principal vectors. So both methods remove the same direction every iteration.
  • Takeaway: SPV is the geometric view of RFB-EDMD, so it inherits RFB-EDMD's guarantee that exact eigenfunctions are retained.

9Numerical drift in large dictionaries

  • Experiment: a system with four known eigenfunctions, embedded in a large dictionary. Track the distance to those eigenfunctions as pruning proceeds (read the plot right to left).
  • SPV loses them by the end, even though in exact arithmetic it should keep them. The cause is finite-precision error.
  • The teaser already shows this plot, so the section can point to it instead of repeating it.

10Characterizing the drift

  • Setting: an eigenfunction that is only ε-close to the initial dictionary, which is always the case in finite precision.
  • Bound: after T steps, dist(f, ST) ≤ ε ∏k √(1 + C̃2/γk2), where γk is the smallest sine among directions removed at step k.
  • Design lessons: reduce the number of steps T, and keep each γk large.

11MPV-SPV: coarse, then fine

  • Algorithm: MPV with εcoarse = 0.1 removes only the clearly bad directions in a few steps, then SPV with ε = 0.001 refines one direction at a time.
  • Connect it to the bound: MPV cuts T, and the coarse threshold keeps γk large.
  • Output: a nested sequence S0 ⊃ S1 ⊃ … ⊃ ST, which is used later to choose a subspace.
  • Results: about 4 orders of magnitude closer to the eigenfunctions and a final subspace about 20× closer to invariant (the teaser figures).

12Fast pruning with rank-one updates

  • Goal: update the principal angles and vectors after dropping k vectors, without recomputing from scratch.
  • Pipeline: QR of the Koopman image, a chain of k rank-one eigen-updates at O(s2) each, then rotate W and R. No new pass over the data is needed.
  • Cost per step: O(ks2) instead of O(s3).

14From a pruned subspace to a model

  • Two competing goals: accuracy (εacc, the worst-case relative prediction error) and expressivity (εexp, how well the state is captured by S). The trivial subspace span{1} is exactly invariant but captures nothing.
  • Choosing along the pruning sequence: early subspaces reconstruct the state better and suit short horizons; late subspaces stay accurate over many steps and suit long horizons and planning.
  • For state prediction: lift with the pruned dictionary, evolve with At, then map back through C (from the hidden State Reconstruction slide).

15Example: Van der Pol oscillator

  • Setup: 500 trajectories × 400 steps from [−4, 4]2, Δt = 0.025. Dictionary of 289 Wendland-kernel RBFs on a 0.5 grid. MPV-SPV with εcoarse = 0.1, then ε = 0.01, keeps a 12-dimensional subspace. Baselines are EDMD and kernel EDMD on all 289 functions.
  • Result: the 12-dimensional model tracks the oscillator over 3000 steps. Kernel EDMD is better early but goes unstable, EDMD drifts, and the lifted-state error stays orders of magnitude lower.
  • Swap the slide for the four prediction and error plots from the paper.

16Bigger is not always better

Damped Duffing oscillator: the leading non-trivial eigenfunction (eigenvalue 1) from the full 503-element dictionary (left) and from a 14-dimensional pruned subspace (right). Only the pruned one separates the two basins of attraction.
  • An eigenfunction with eigenvalue 1 should be constant on each basin of attraction, so it is a direct test of eigenfunction quality.
  • The full dictionary gives a noisy, local blob. The 14-dimensional pruned subspace cleanly separates the two basins.
  • Link forward to the OPAL project, which builds on this idea.

17Summary

  • Takeaways: a geometric unification of pruning algorithms, numerical drift explained and fixed, fast pruning, models for prediction, and better eigenfunctions.
  • Next steps: extend to RKHS (link to the kernel pruning page, which does this), exploit eigenfunction quality, balance expressivity against accuracy, and pruning for Koopman control families. Keep only the directions you want public.
